MTC™ Diagnostic Clinical Question Simulator

Test your clinical reasoning against this live sample vignette audited under the dual-blind Mechanism-to-Clinic (MTC™) framework:

A 54-year-old male presents to the emergency room with 2 hours of crushing retrosternal chest pain radiating to his left jaw. His blood pressure is 85/55 mmHg, pulse is 115 bpm, and oxygen saturation is 91% on room air. The 12-lead ECG demonstrates ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF with clear ST depression in leads I and aVL. Which of the following is the most critical immediate management step?
A. Administer Sublingual Nitroglycerin 0.4 mg immediately
B. Rapid Normal Saline Bolus (500 mL) & Urgent Primary PCI Activation
C. Administer Intravenous Metoprolol 5 mg slowly
D. Immediate Intravenous Furosemide 40 mg bolus
✓ MTC™ Clinical Rationale: The patient has an acute Inferior Wall STEMI (leads II, III, aVF) complicated by Right Ventricular Infarction (hypotension, tachycardia, clear lungs). Right ventricular stroke volume is preload-dependent. Nitrates and beta-blockers are contraindicated because they reduce preload and precipitate severe cardiovascular collapse. The correct initial response is volume expansion (Saline bolus) to maintain right ventricular filling while activating primary reperfusion (PCI).
